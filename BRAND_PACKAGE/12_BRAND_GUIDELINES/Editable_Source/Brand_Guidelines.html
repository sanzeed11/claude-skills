1.1 · The one idea

Carry the light across.

The brand idea, derived from confirmed copy on sanzeed.com and recorded in 01_BRAND_STRATEGY/Brand_Strategy.md §1.

The site already says it: “Between two points of light. A brief on one side, a visitor on the other. Seven ways to carry the light across.” A client arrives with an intention; a visitor leaves with a memory. Everything in between — the story, the room, the light, the timing, the team — is carried across without dropping it.

Every part of this identity serves that idea. The system is built from three things, and only three:

The point
The gold dot — the point of light. The brand’s smallest unit and its most recognisable: it sits in the wordmark, ends the signature (“Sanzeed.”), closes headlines (“lit.”) and becomes the symbol.
The beam
The hairline the point travels along. The route through a museum, the timeline of a career, the rule in a document.
The room
The dark field everything sits in. Black is not a background colour here; it is the gallery before the lights come up.

4.1 · Personality and voice

Five traits, seven principles.

Archetype: the Creator tempered by the Sage. Use this page as the pre-flight check for any piece of writing or design.

Calm.

Space, one accent, short sentences. Nothing shouts.

Precise.

Numbered steps, named deliverables, 001 counters, wall labels.

Monumental.

A large light serif, a single object on a dark field.

Warm.

Gold light, first person, “the first conversation is always free”.

Curious.

“Every environment begins as a question.”

The voice in one line

A museum’s wall label, written by the person who built the room.

Seven principles
  1. First person, singular. “I”, “me”, “my”. The brand is one person.
  2. Short sentences, long ideas. One thought per sentence. Let a full stop land.
  3. Name the deliverable. Say what the client receives, concretely.
  4. Spatial vocabulary. Rooms, routes, thresholds, galleries, light, dwell time, opening night.
  5. End on the image. Finish paragraphs on a picture, not a claim.
  6. Plain numbers. Numerals, no inflation. “90 projects, one continuous craft.”
  7. Warm, not casual. Hospitable; never slangy or jokey.
Three tests for any new copy. 1 · Could this be read aloud, calmly, in a museum lobby? 2 · Does it name a room, a route, a light or a deliverable? 3 · Would deleting the adjectives make it stronger? If yes, delete them.

Clear space & placement.

x = half the cap height of the logo in use, on all sides. Nothing enters it: no text, no edge, no other logo, no photographic detail.

  1. 8.1Clear space
  2. 8.2Placement
Sanzeed Mirza · Experiential Design ConsultantBrand Guidelines · Version 1.0 · November 2026
8.1 · Clear space

Half the cap height, all round.

The clear-space sheet from 02_LOGO_SYSTEM/Guidelines, drawn for the primary wordmark, the horizontal-descriptor lockup, the vertical stack and the symbol.

The measure. x is half the height of the wordmark’s capitals in the lockup you are using. For the symbol on its own, x is a quarter of its height. For the stacked lockup, x is measured from the wordmark inside it.

What may not enter. Text, rules, edges of the page or screen, other logos, partner marks, photographic detail, pattern above 12 %.

What may. Flat brand colour, the lamp glow, the veil, grain. The glow in motion stays inside the clear space.

Margins. On documents the lockup sits at the 20 mm margin; the clear space is then automatically satisfied for a 56 mm lockup. On screens the nav wordmark at 18 px cap height carries 9 px of clear space built into its padding.

8.2 · Placement

Top-left, or centred.

The placement sheet shows the approved positions on a document, a slide, a social post and a cover.

  1. Top-left or centred. The logo anchors a composition from the top-left (documents, website, social banners) or from the centre (covers, end cards, square tiles). Never bottom-right unless it is the sign-off on a cover, where it may sit bottom-left or centred with the lamp behind.
  2. Documents. The horizontal-descriptor lockup sits top-left at the 20 mm margin, 56 mm wide on A4; continuation pages carry the primary wordmark at 30 mm.
  3. Covers. The primary wordmark sits bottom-left or centred with the lamp glow behind it; the title sits above, never beside.
  4. Photography. The logo sits on the darkest third, under the veil, on ≥ 80 % black. Measure it; do not guess.
  5. With partners. Equal cap height, separated by a hairline with 2x between marks; Sanzeed’s mark first when host, last when guest.
  6. Signage. Centre line 1500 mm above finished floor for door plates; the wordmark centre 1700 mm on backdrops.

Chapter10

Incorrect usage.

Twelve things never done to the logo. If a layout needs any of them, the layout is wrong, not the logo.

  1. 10.1The twelve
Sanzeed Mirza · Experiential Design ConsultantBrand Guidelines · Version 1.0 · November 2026
10.1 · The twelve

Twelve ways to break it.

The sheet from 02_LOGO_SYSTEM/Guidelines; each tile shows the error on the background where it is most tempting.

  1. Do not stretch or squash. Scale uniformly only.
  2. Do not rotate. Horizontal, always; the stacked lockup for vertical spaces.
  3. Do not outline. The letters are solid; the stroke is the drawing, not a border.
  4. Do not add shadows, bevels, glows or effects to the letters. Only the points may glow, and only in motion.
  5. Do not recolour the points of light. Gold, or the ink of a single-colour version.
  6. Do not typeset the name instead of the wordmark, in any typeface.
  7. Do not use the light version on dark, or
  8. the dark version on light.
  9. Do not place on busy photography without the veil.
  10. Do not place the full-colour logo on gold.
  11. Do not fill with gradients, textures or imagery.
  12. Do not crop the wordmark or the S.

Also never: change the letter spacing, pair with the old raster signature, set the descriptor in another font or case, animate the letters by stroke-drawing or particles, or redraw any letter by hand.

11.1 · The signature mark

The personal layer.

Hand-lettered “Sanzeed.” vectorised from the original artwork (potrace, 300 dpi, editable Béziers). Five colours, 02_LOGO_SYSTEM/Signature.

  1. Personal, not institutional. Letters, thank-you cards, sign-offs, the end of a case study. Never on invoices, proposals, signage or in place of the wordmark.
  2. Minimum 32 mm in print, 160 px on screen. Below that the flourish closes up.
  3. Keep the flourish underline. Never recolour parts of it, never separate the dot.
  4. Vector only. The traced SVG/PDF/EPS from 02_LOGO_SYSTEM/Signature; the original raster signature is retired.
  5. Sign-off form. “— Sanzeed Mirza” under quotes; the signature mark alone reads “Sanzeed.”
11.2 · The monogram

SM. — the seal.

Sanzeed Mirza’s initials in the construction of the wordmark, with the point as the full stop. Seven colourways like every other variant.

  1. Seals and embossing. Blind deboss or foil on card stock, wax-seal scale.
  2. ID-card reverse. 16 mm, with the QR code and contact lines (09_PRINT_STATIONERY/ID_Card).
  3. Pattern. The monogram-repeat tile at ≤ 12 % opacity (05_GRAPHIC_ASSETS/Patterns).
  4. Never as the primary mark. The monogram does not replace the symbol on avatars or favicons; it is the mark where “SM.” is read as initials.

16.1 · Photography style

The lit room.

The style in four pictures and six rules, from Photography_Guidelines.pdf (16 pages, 06_PHOTOGRAPHY_ART_DIRECTION) and the Moodboard.

  1. One light source. The exhibit, the projection or the lamp lights the room; no flash, no fill that flattens.
  2. Deep, open blacks. Shadows land on Night #0D0D0F with detail; never crushed, never lifted to grey.
  3. Warm highlights. The highlight band leans toward Lamp Gold; cool secondary light sits ≤ 1 stop under.
  4. People for scale. Visitors walking into the light, backs or profiles, never posed at camera.
  5. One-point perspective along the route; thirds for people; symmetry for architecture.
  6. Every picture carries a wall label (Title · Place) and sits in the room or under the veil.

16.6 · Portrait standards

One warm light, eye line at a third.

The approved portrait in four ratios (06_PHOTOGRAPHY_ART_DIRECTION/Approved_Images/Portrait; press headshots in 10_MARKETING_COLLATERAL/Press).

  1. Warm key, bokeh behind, black garment. The existing portrait sets the standard: one warm light, points of light out of focus, the subject in black.
  2. Eye line in the upper third (35–38 % of the height) in every crop.
  3. Night behind. The background continues to #0D0D0F; never a white or grey studio sweep.
  4. No retouching beyond the grade. No skin smoothing, no teeth, no background swaps.
  5. New portraits follow the same brief and are graded with grade.py before use.

23.1 · Principles and the timing scale

Light, not bounce.

Light has no mass. It does not overshoot, spring, wobble, spin or stretch. It arrives quickly and settles slowly; it glows; it leaves without a sound. From 11_MOTION_GRAPHICS/Motion_Principles.md.

TokenDurationUse
--t-fast200 msA point appears, a word arrives, a hover responds, a stroke fades after a fill
--t400 msThe standard entrance: a line rises, a label settles, a letter blooms, an element sinks out
--t-slow800 msThe lamp rises, a beam grows, a glow pulses once, a plate wipes in
--t-reveal1100 msThe reveal: the point travels the full beam, the light wipes a quote, photography settles

Staggers: words 160 ms apart, lines 150 ms; the four steps of the route light when the light reaches them. In a 4 s reveal 1.2 s is hold; in a 5 s lower third 2.9 s is hold.

  1. One thing moves. A composition has one travelling element at a time — the point, the light, a line arriving. Letters, labels and lines follow the light.
  2. Left → right, top → down. The beam travels in reading direction; the lamp rises from above. Nothing enters from the right; nothing moves backwards.
  3. Arrive fast, settle long. The first 200 ms do most of the work; the rest is a settle the eye can rest on.
  4. Reveal by light. Things are not animated in; they are lit. A letter appears because the point passed it.
  5. Glow is gold and belongs to the point. Letters, type and photographs never glow, blur or cast coloured light.
  6. Holds are calm. Once arrived, nothing moves for at least 1.2 s before a piece ends. No idle motion, no floating, no slow zoom.
  7. Exits are quieter than entrances. 400 ms on --ease-in with a 12 px sink. Nothing flies out.
  8. Real copy, real rooms. Wall labels on every image; sentence-case headlines; one lit word at most.
23.2 · Easing curves
