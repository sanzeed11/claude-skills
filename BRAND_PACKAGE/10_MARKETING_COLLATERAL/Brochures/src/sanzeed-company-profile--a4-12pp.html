Sanzeed Mirza started where light is made by hand: in VFX suites and concept-art studios, drawing the frames that brands and films would later build. For five years he learned how a single image carries an idea.

Then the frame became a room. From 2018 he directed museums, experience centres, pavilions and shows at national scale — the Prime Minister Museum in New Delhi, where visitors walk a tricolour corridor and meet one leader at a time; the Ram Katha Show in Ayodhya, where the script came first and every surface carried a line of it; Buddha Park in Uttarakhand, where the walk itself is the meditation.

Ninety projects later, the method is simple to say and hard to do: discover the one central story, direct a single vision across every discipline, craft space, light, motion and interaction into a room, and deliver it — built, tested and opened. In 2026 he began working independently, so that institutions and brands can bring the whole room to one person.

Case study · 001 · Museums & Experience Centres

Prime Minister Museum.

New Delhi

The brief

One museum for every Prime Minister of India since 1947, in New Delhi — a building that has to hold schoolchildren and heads of state on the same afternoon, and give each leader a room without turning the whole into a corridor of portraits.

The idea

A corridor you walk in order.

The spine of the museum is a single long passage under a tricolour canopy. Visitors meet one Prime Minister at a time, in sequence, so the building itself tells the story of the office before any gallery does. Portraits face you at eye level; the leaders’ own words sit beside them on the walls.

The walk

Off the spine, each gallery changes its mood to its subject: wood and light for one era, screens and sound for another. Guides stand at the thresholds; planting softens the floor so the passage reads as a walk rather than a hall.

What visitors do

Visitors walk the whole line of leaders without a map, and come out knowing the order by heart.

Case study · 002 · Projection Mapping & Shows

Ram Katha Show.

Ayodhya

The brief

A night show for Ayodhya that tells the Ram Katha without a stage or actors — the audience is seated in front of a great hall, and the story has to come out of the architecture itself.

The idea

The building is the screen.

Every dome, arch and column of the hall was mapped and painted with light, so the story seems to rise from the stone. Scenes move from the central shrine outwards along the galleries; the gilded ceiling becomes sky, then forest, then fire.

Ram Katha Show·Ayodhya·007 The whole hall lit as a court
08Sanzeed Mirza · Experiential Design Consultant
001A field of lamps opens the night
002The name, written in light

The walk

Projection, sound and timing were composed as one score: the beats of the narration cue the light, and the architecture’s own rhythm (arch after arch) sets the pace of the scenes.

What visitors do

Visitors sit in the dark and watch a building they know by day become the story by night.

Case study · 003 · Museums & Experience Centres

Buddha Park.

Uttarakhand

The brief

A park in Uttarakhand around a reclining Buddha, with a stupa, shrines and gardens — a landscape that must work as a place of calm for pilgrims and a day out for families.

The idea

A garden of gestures.

The park is laid out as one loop: from the gate past the shrines to the stupa, around the reclining Buddha and back to a fountain court ringed by sculpted hands in the mudras. The walk itself is the meditation; nothing is read, everything is passed.

Buddha Park·Uttarakhand·008 The Buddha at full scale, at the end of the walk
10Sanzeed Mirza · Experiential Design Consultant
002The fountain court from above, the Buddha resting beyond
003Under the pavilion: benches, lamps and shade

The walk

Water, stone and planting carry the route. The Buddha rests against a green hillside so it is seen first from a distance and only slowly at full scale; the fountain court gives the loop a place to pause.

What visitors do

Visitors complete the loop at their own pace and end at the water, facing the Buddha.
